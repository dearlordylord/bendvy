# Independent semantic audit of the replacement laws

The replacement is materially stronger than the withdrawn fourteen-law bundle. It contains meaningful bounded ECS observations, independent command semantics and real runtime caller equations. It should **not be presented as 31 public ECS contracts**. My recommendation is to present **7 bounded public-observation candidates**, with **20 separately identified internal lemmas** and **4 encoding/arithmetic infrastructure candidates**. Approval remains a human decision; no law or proof is approved here.

Reviewed subject: `experiments/t11-replacement/LAWS.bend`, SHA256 `e0c607d723ad250a189c096d9fa3fd6a36913f4c72efd3a3be6dacd7fadb2cbc`. Governing sources: [SPEC](../SPEC.md), [issue #12](https://github.com/dearlordylord/bendvy/issues/12), [prior decision](laws-decision.md), [proposal](laws-replacement-package.md), and the experiment's README, DESIGN, types, model, independent spec, runtime and owned spec. This is a fresh source-level semantic review, not a replay of its falsification suite. No concrete false equation was found; that is not a proof of truth. The broader authority, payload and transaction claims discussed below remain insufficient/deferred even if every equation is true.

## Every candidate

**Public** means meaningful at the explicitly bounded value/tag model or runtime observation seam, not an approved production API. **Internal** means useful support or an experimental implementation contract, not a standalone public ECS guarantee. **Infrastructure** means an encoding/arithmetic fact. ## Oracle, domains and composition

The oracle is substantively independent where it matters most: `S.query` enumerates `[0,next)` and selects by per-ID lookup, whereas `M.query` sorts physical rows; `S.flush` replays commands independently for each slot, whereas the implementation mutates an entire row list. Completeness, survivor values, exact FIFO and queue clearing are consequently meaningful constraints, not the same `apply_all` expression on both sides. Creation/reservation/publication specs are necessarily close structural restatements of the prototype; these are policy/implementation facts rather than independent justification of the policy.

Admissibility is an explicit overapproximation: it permits slot 0, arbitrary namespace numbers, counters below the normal creation start and nonspawn targets outside the allocated range. It excludes duplicate live rows and pending/live spawn overlap. These choices support the stated equations and are not demonstrated false domains. Reachable positive fixtures show some inhabited cases; they do not establish that every admissible world is reachable, which is unnecessary for a stronger theorem over this overapproximation. Conversely, no law should be advertised as protecting arbitrary malformed or externally forged states merely because some functions have total equations.

The U32 boundary is handled coherently for the covered operations. Actual metadata and inputs are already U32; successful creation/reservation increment only below a U32 limit, and `step_safe` excludes increment of a live MAX payload. `run_safe` rechecks each independent prefix, including terminal admissibility; initial safety alone would not suffice. Overflowing runtime schedules are excluded, not rejected by a proved public API. MAX-boundary compiled controls are useful but do not prove either bridge equation. Bridge functions and inline runtime arithmetic still need a checked connection.

There is no direct creation-admissibility or runtime-admissibility-preservation theorem in the 31 IDs. These are required proof links, not an identified false equation: creation exactness, model preservation, projection exactness and faithful sorted observation make such links plausible to derive. A future trace proof must establish them explicitly, together with observation adequacy (sorting preserves duplicates/fields and does not hide an invalid row). Raw `O.snapshot` preserves physical row order, while `M.observe` sorts it; the README's relation must distinguish structural equality from public observational equality. Step correspondence does not establish raw-list identity with `S.step`, whose flush/bump materialize ascending rows. A proof must use the appropriate relation rather than silently treating these as the same equality.

## What all 31 still allow

1. **Independent-root collision.** Construct two `R.Factory{0}` values, create worlds through each, reserve/flush their first entity and use the first world's handle in the second. Both namespaces/local IDs coincide, so lookup can succeed in the second world while all equations retain their stated truth. Affineness prevents duplicating one factory; it does not prevent constructing two roots. This is a missing global authority boundary, already acknowledged in the package, and blocks promoting these laws to the full approved foreign-world guarantee.
2. **Unobserved payload loss.** A query/projection returning newly allocated one-element arrays with the same element-zero values can satisfy the observational preservation equations while discarding every other element. The erased quantifier admits such larger arrays. Erasure permits repeated mathematical mention of an owner; it neither clones a live owner nor strengthens the observation to all payload contents. Base arrays have nonempty leaf/node constructors, so element zero is defined; a one-element shape invariant is not encoded by the quantifier.
3. **Broken reader transactions.** Every helper can be correct while the system wrapper selects the wrong reader, passes the wrong boundary, advances after failure, publishes failed messages or changes another reader. The helpers are easy defining equations, useful as internal contracts only. Their successful mutants do not establish independent cursors, retention/lag, rollback or retry behavior.
4. **Wrong production integration.** Correct primitive `R.tick` does not constrain an external scheduler/provider or a future storage implementation that never calls it. Query and lookup do link their actual runtime callers, although they intentionally reuse the model query/search; step and tick use separate owned transitions. This is genuine linkage within the slice, not universal caller coverage.

Raw `T.World` equalities in reserve/publication and raw owner snapshots in read laws freeze physical row order; numeric namespace/counter equalities freeze an allocation strategy. Public ordered queries and logical FIFO are justified observables. Internal physical order and contiguous allocation are not automatically public requirements. Before applying these laws to a new layout or allocator, replace the relevant structural equality with an explicit representation-neutral relation; do not weaken the required query order to make optimization easier.

## Reference semantics and recommendation

Pinned TS `packages/core/src/internal/queries.ts:398-411,435-456` sorts by ID. `Runtime.ts:1387-1447` distinguishes condition skip, failure rollback, successful cursor advancement/publication and explicit FIFO application. The primitive model correctly captures the selected query/command order, but omits the transaction wrapper. Rust Bevy's explicit query sorting adapters and configurable final-deferred application (`crates/bevy_ecs/src/query/iter.rs`, `schedule/executor/single_threaded.rs:160-173`) do not replace those TS requirements. The foreign lookup divergence is approved by SPEC; independent-root provenance is still unestablished. Foreign publication's silent no-op and bounded allocation/rejection remain experimental policy, not discovered TS parity.

**Present now for bounded public-observation approval:** `query_any_complete_ordered`, `query_present_complete_ordered`, `query_absent_complete_ordered`, `lookup_full_exact`, `explicit_flush_independent`, `schedule_execution_exact`, `owned_runtime_schedule_correspondence` at the exact hash above. Their approval text must retain the single-composition scope, admissibility/prefix domain, element-zero observation and primitive-step meaning of schedule. These seven have meaningful public behavior as their central subject; metadata and allocation choices still belong to this bounded model, not a final production contract.

The other **20 Internal** and **4 Infrastructure** IDs may be presented simultaneously for separately labeled supporting-proof approval. Approval of creation/reservation/publication equations must say “facts about this experimental bounded interpreter,” without adopting their allocator/error policy for the product. Actual owned query/lookup result clauses are valuable, but their complete statements are exact prototype refinement: a read that compacts or reorders physical rows while preserving every public result fails their raw `O.snapshot` equality. They need a representation-neutral owner relation before being presented as public preservation laws. `reservation_pending_lookup` needs a direct successful-reservation/lookup observation instead of an independently replaceable Missing observer. These are concrete classification/revision requirements, not limits that disappear because the README already mentions them.

Do not count the five cursor helpers or two conditional encoder equations as seven newly established public ECS behaviors. No source-discovered false law requires immediate withdrawal of the whole replacement; several true equations still need changed subjects or narrower approval labels.

**Block broader claims, not bounded research:** establish unforgeable root lineage before global authority acceptance; define a full payload relation before arbitrary Type preservation; add actual selected-reader/transaction transitions before reader capability acceptance; settle production exhaustion and foreign-command results before declaring their policy final; discharge the creation/preservation/observation links before universal runtime-refinement claims. None of these requires modeling the entire future ECS before this bounded subset can be reviewed.
